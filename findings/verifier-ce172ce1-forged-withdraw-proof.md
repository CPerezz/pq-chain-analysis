## What happens

Tornado Cash's 19 classic pools pay whoever presents a proof this verifier accepts; the four ETH pools hold 306,226.2 ETH. Its key is fixed in the code, and no pool can switch verifier. A quantum computer could derive the setup secret from that key and forge proofs that empty the pools.

## Evidence

- [verifyProof](https://etherscan.io/address/0xce172ce1f20ec0b3728c9965470eaf994a03557a#code) checks a Groth16 proof with a BN254 pairing against a verifying key written into the code.
- In the 100 ETH pool, [withdraw](https://etherscan.io/address/0xa160cdab225685da1d56aa342ad8841c3b53f291#code) pays 100 ETH once this verifier accepts the proof; the pool holds 278,000 ETH.
- The older pools' [operator](https://etherscan.io/address/0xa160cdab225685da1d56aa342ad8841c3b53f291#readContract), who could change verifier, is the zero address; newer pools fix it at deployment.

## Reviewer notes

### Mechanism

Each pool's withdraw (TornadoCash_eth.sol 248-255 in the 100 ETH pool; ERC20Tornado.sol 302-323 in the token pools) checks that the fee is within the denomination, the nullifier hash is unspent and the root is a known recent one, then requires verifier.verifyProof(proof, [root, nullifierHash, recipient, relayer, fee, refund]), marks the nullifier spent and pays the denomination to the recipient. verifyProof (Verifier.sol 187-224) builds vk_x from the hard-coded IC points with precompiles 6 and 7 and runs the pairing check (precompile 8, line 138) against alfa1, beta2, gamma2, delta2, all constants of the pure function verifyingKey (168-181). With the setup trapdoor, which a quantum computer derives from these public points, a proof verifies for any public inputs, so a fresh nullifier hash and any recipient pass; repeated, this drains each pool. The 11 older pools could repoint verifier only via updateVerifier (onlyOperator), and their operator is the zero address; the 8 newer ERC20Tornado/cTornado pools store verifier as immutable and have no updateVerifier. No pool has an EIP-1967 implementation. The 100 ETH pool's 278,000 ETH equals 2,780 unspent 100 ETH deposits, each controlled by its own note holder.

### Checks

- Header facts: this address alone, at block 26,438,999.
- Ruled out: unused code: all 19 pools in Tornado's instance registry return this address from verifier() at the block.
- Ruled out: an admin path on the verifier: its only external function is verifyProof; the key is constants in a pure function, no owner, no proxy (tool facts).
- Ruled out: a pool that can repoint: 11 pools have operator() = 0x0 (onlyOperator guards updateVerifier, changeOperator and, in the 0.1 ETH pool, migrateState); 8 pools have an immutable verifier and no updateVerifier selector; EIP-1967 implementation slot empty on all 19.
- Ruled out: proxying in the pools: disassembly shows no DELEGATECALL in the 1, 10 and 100 ETH pools, 0x23773e (100k DAI) and 0x03893a (cDAI); the 0.1 ETH pool's two DELEGATECALLs go to the constant Hasher library 0x83584f83f26af4edda9cbe8c730bc87c364b28fe (MiMCSponge).
- Ruled out: value: the verifier itself holds nothing; the money at stake is the pools' (users' deposits). Token pool balances (DAI, cDAI, USDC, USDT, WBTC) were not re-read here; the card counts only the four ETH pools.
- Ruled out: EIP-8151: the verifier makes no ecrecover call; nothing here depends on it.
